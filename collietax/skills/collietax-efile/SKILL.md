# CollieTax e-file

CollieTax prepares federal tax returns, and state ones, from the user's records. You read their records and fill the facts; CollieTax's `prepare_return` (on the CollieTax MCP server, https://collietax.com/api/mcp) computes every line and checks IRS business rules. One tool for every form, which its `form` names; read a form with `describe_form` before filling it: its fields, how many returns a call and a user hold, its deadlines, the documents it is read from and what confirming does. The forms: `941` (Form 941, 2025, 2026 and 2027 quarters) and `941-X` (2025, 2026 and 2027 quarters); `940` (Form 940, tax year 2026); `7004` and `4868` (tax year 2026); `94x PIN` (a business's 94x Online Signature PIN Registration, which asks IRS for the 10-digit PIN its owner or principal then signs the business's own Forms 941 and 940 with); `990-N` (a small tax-exempt organization's Form 990-N, the e-Postcard, tax years beginning in 2026, filed by the organization itself); `990-EZ` (a tax-exempt organization's own Form 990-EZ, keyed by IRS's element names, each of its schedules read first with `describe_form` and that `schedule`, tax years beginning in 2026, which its officer signs on Form 8453-TE on the review page); `8868` (an exempt organization's own Form 8868, the 6-month extension of its Form 990, 990-EZ, 990-PF, 990-T, 4720, 1041-A, 5227 or 6069, tax years beginning in 2026, signed (Form 8453-TE, by its officer) only when a payment goes with it); `W-2` (an employer's Forms W-2 and W-3, wages paid in 2026) and `W-2c` (Forms W-2c and W-3c, correcting 2024-2026 Forms W-2), at most 30 employees; `1094-C` (an Applicable Large Employer's Form 1094-C and Forms 1095-C, 2026 coverage, at most 30 Forms 1095-C; Forms 1094-B and 1095-B are not supported); a payer's `1099-NEC` (pay for a contractor's services), `1099-MISC` (rents, royalties, prizes, an attorney's gross proceeds), `1099-INT` (interest paid), `1099-DIV` (a corporation's dividends) and `1099-R` (distributions from a pension, profit-sharing or retirement plan, an IRA or an annuity), payments made in 2026, at most 30 recipients per form (the other Forms 1099 are not supported); `1099-S` (real estate closings in 2026, at most 30 sellers), `1099-C` and `1099-A` (debts canceled and property acquired or abandoned in 2026), `1098` (mortgage interest received in 2026), at most 30 people per form, and a corporation's `3921` (ISO exercises in 2026) and `3922` (first transfers in 2026 of ESPP shares), at most 30 employees per form. A state form's name names the state: `CA-DE9` (California's DE 9 with its DE 9C, 2026 quarters, every employee of the quarter in one call, at most 200); `CA-DE34` (the DE 34, Report of New Employee(s)) and `CA-DE542` (the DE 542, Report of Independent Contractor(s)), each due within 20 days, at most 200 people per report; `CA-DE88` (the DE 88, Payroll Tax Deposit: one deposit EDD debits from the employer's bank account, SDI and PIT or UI and ETT). Before gathering records, tell the user when the year they ask about is not one of these, or when an employer has more than 30 employees for Forms W-2 or 1095-C, or a payer more than 30 recipients of one Form 1099. Every `prepare_return` call saves one company's returns of one form as one batch of drafts, which the user reads as the printed forms on the batch's review page and confirms there. On that page a business filing its own Form 941, 941-X or 940 also signs it: the signer signs a Form 8453-EMP for it, the declaration the IRS receives with the return. You never sign or confirm for the user. A tax professional's client signs with a PIN instead (below). What confirming does depends on where the form is filed, and `describe_form` says it for each form: an IRS form's batch is sent to the IRS, and `get_return_batch` reads its transmission and the agency's verdict; for Forms W-2 and W-2c, the user downloads the file on the review page and uploads it to SSA themselves, in Business Services Online (BSO); a California form is not sent to EDD yet, and waits, confirmed. A batch with `test` true is never filed: an IRS batch goes only to the IRS's test system, even when the IRS accepts it, and SSA has no test system, so a test batch's W-2 file must never be uploaded in BSO, where any file is a real filing. Say so whenever you tell the user a test batch was sent or accepted, or give them its review link. Confirming holds each charged form's price from the user's credits, dollars they add ahead of time (one credit is one dollar), which is what their pages call the balance. The price is spent only once the agency accepts the form, and comes back if it is rejected or the batch is withdrawn. With too few credits the page cannot confirm. A draft from `prepare_return` or `get_return_batch` gives its `cost`. When the credits cannot cover it, the review page beside Confirm shows the way on (add credits, pay an open payment, or wait for one clearing), so give the user the batch's `review_url`. `get_billing` reads the user's credits (its `balance`) and payments as their Billing page shows them, with that page's `url`. A draft's cost, the credits and links come from the tools, never worked out; before there is a draft, an estimate comes from the pricing page (What it costs, below).

If calling a CollieTax tool fails with HTTP 404 instead of returning a tool result, or a CollieTax tool that a CollieTax skill names is missing from your tools, this app holds an old CollieTax plugin or tool list. A NOT_FOUND inside a tool result, a failed file upload, or the customer declining a tool is not this. Update the CollieTax plugin as below, tell the customer to reconnect CollieTax (in Claude Code from /mcp, in Codex by starting a new session, in claude.ai and Cowork on the plugin's Connectors tab), then stop.

These CollieTax skills are version 0.5.3 of the CollieTax plugin. When a CollieTax tool result carries a `skills_version` higher than 0.5.3, compared as MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH numbers, a newer plugin exists: once in the conversation, update it as below, then carry on with these skills. A version equal to or lower than 0.5.3 needs nothing.

To update the CollieTax plugin, do the steps you can, and tell the customer, once and in one short message, the steps only they can do, never in a scheduled run: in Claude Code, when claude plugin list shows collietax@synced and no other collietax, the plugin comes from the customer's claude.ai account: it updates there, as below, and reaches Claude Code when the customer starts it again; when it shows collietax@collietax, run claude plugin marketplace update collietax, then claude plugin update collietax@collietax, and the customer runs /reload-plugins; when it shows collietax@<marketplace> for another marketplace, such as the Claude Directory's, run claude plugin marketplace update <marketplace>, then claude plugin update collietax@<marketplace>, and the customer runs /reload-plugins; in Codex, run codex plugin marketplace upgrade collietax, then codex plugin add collietax@collietax, and the new version loads in the next Codex session; in claude.ai and Cowork, a plugin added from the Claude Directory updates by itself once the directory publishes the new version, which can come a little after CollieTax names it, and one added from the collietax marketplace updates when the customer selects Check for updates on that marketplace in Customize > Plugins, or by itself once they turn on Sync automatically there.

When the customer asks to reach the CollieTax team (a question, a problem or a request), or a CollieTax tool keeps failing and they agree to report it, send a support request with `contact_support`; the team answers by email.

## What it costs

Prices: when the customer asks what a CollieTax service costs (bookkeeping, a tax return, payroll, or forms they file themselves), read CollieTax's price list at https://collietax.com/pricing.md (Markdown, made for you to read) and answer from it, never from memory or an earlier conversation. The link to give the customer is https://collietax.com/pricing; if you cannot open the list, give them that link instead of a price. First give the starting price in one line, then offer a closer estimate from a few questions, asked together in plain words:
- A tax return: (1) whose return, and for which years (each year is a return of its own): their own (and their spouse's), a company (how many owners, whether it elected to be an S-corp or C-corp, whether an owner lives outside the US), a trust or estate, or a nonprofit; when they do not know the company's type, the form number at the top left of last year's return; (2) the business's revenue for the year, roughly, and whether its books for the year are done; (3) every state, and city with its own income tax, they lived in, earned income in or did business in; (4) which of these applied, and how many: rental properties, real estate or business property sold, brokerage accounts, K-1s the owner received, foreign accounts, subsidiaries, owners or gifts, a tax notice to answer, and for a company, the owners whose own returns they want done too. CollieTax does not take a return with only W-2 income: say so.
- Bookkeeping: whether they keep the books in their own AI agent or want our bookkeeper; about how many transactions a month, and for our bookkeeper, how much money moves in and out a month and whether they need accrual books or their own QuickBooks Online; any earlier months or years to catch up.
- Payroll we run: how many people are paid, whether only the owners, and in which states.
- Forms they file themselves: which forms, and how many of each (one per person for W-2s and 1099s, one per quarter for a 941 or DE 9).
Read the list as it is written: use the column of their plan (a single-member LLC's, a partnership or S-corp's, a C-corp's); where a line says how many are included, only the ones past that cost extra; the K-1 line counts K-1s received on a single-member LLC's return and, for a partnership or S-corp, the K-1s it issues, one per owner; shares sold in a brokerage account are that account's line, not property sold; a plan with tiers costs the price of the first tier that covers both their transactions and the money they move a month. Add the prices for what they told you and give the total as a starting estimate, naming each part and checking that the parts add up to it, such as "about $4,749: $1,899 for the S-corp, $450 for New York, $400 for the rental and $1,000 for each of its 2 owners' own returns". Say it is an estimate: the final price is set by what their documents show (a tax return's by the CPA, under the price list the engagement letter attaches), and government taxes and filing fees are extra. Where the page says custom quote (a trust, estate or gift return, a business over $500,000 in revenue or a complex one, volume past the top tier), say it is priced after a look rather than guessing. Use these answers later instead of asking them again.

## A tax professional filing for clients

When the user is a tax professional (a CPA, enrolled agent or other ERO) e-filing a client's Form 941, 941-X or 940 under their own IRS e-file application, ask them once for their registration and save it with `register_tax_professional`: their name and PTIN, whether they are self-employed, their firm's name, EIN, address and phone, their EFIN and 5-digit Practitioner PIN, and, if the IRS accepted them as a Reporting Agent, that EFIN and Reporting Agent PIN (and the firm's name control). Never call it for a business filing its own returns. From then on every Form 941, 941-X and 940 the user prepares is a client's return:
- Ask how the client signs and put it in `signing`: `reporting-agent` when the client authorized them on Form 8655 (leave `signer` out), otherwise `practitioner-pin`, naming the client's officer in `signer`.
- A practitioner-pin return can be prepared before the officer signs Form 8879-EMP: leave `taxpayerPin` and `signedOn` out, give the user the review link, and once the officer has signed Form 8879-EMP, withdraw the batch with `withdraw_batch` and prepare it again with their PIN and the date.
- Tell the user each of the result's `notices` as written: they are the professional's own duties (keeping Form 8879-EMP or Form 8655, and a Reporting Agent's statement to the client). CollieTax never contacts the professional's clients, and nothing of theirs is signed on the review page.
- The other forms (7004, 4868, W-2s, 1099s, California's) are still prepared as the business's own.

## Before the first call

1. Get the records for the period. For a Form 941 or 940: a payroll report or register (for a Form 940, the year's, with FUTA taxable wages), and the federal tax deposits made for it (EFTPS or the payroll provider's tax payment report); for a correction, also the Form 941 as filed. For Forms W-2: the payroll provider's year-end W-2 report (or the year's payroll register with each employee's box amounts), each employee's name and SSN as on their Social Security card and address, and the employer's EIN, name and address as on its Forms 941. For Forms 1094-C / 1095-C: the benefits records (each plan's plan year start month, who was offered what coverage each month, the employee's lowest-cost self-only premium share), the full-time determinations by month, the employee counts by month, and, for a self-insured plan, everyone enrolled. For Forms 1099: the year's payments to each recipient (the ledger's vendor payments or the accounts payable report for contractors and rent; the loan records for interest; the dividend resolutions for dividends; payments by credit card or a payment app are reported by the card company on a Form 1099-K, not here), each recipient's Form W-9 (name, TIN, address, whether they are an individual or a corporation; corporations need no Form 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC except for legal and medical payments), any backup withholding, and the payer's EIN, name and address as on its Forms 941. Contractors' services go on Form 1099-NEC; rent, prizes and a settlement paid through an attorney on Form 1099-MISC; an S corporation's or partnership's distributions are not dividends (they go on Schedule K-1). For Forms 1099-R: the plan's or custodian's distribution records for the year (gross distribution, taxable amount, withholding, the reason for each distribution: normal, early, death, disability, rollover, a designated Roth or Trump account), the recipient's age and the account type (traditional IRA, Roth IRA, SEP, SIMPLE, qualified plan), and each recipient's withholding election (Form W-4R or W-4P). For Forms 1099-S: each closing's Closing Disclosure (or settlement statement) with the contract sales price and the date, each seller's name, TIN and forwarding address (from the seller's certification or Form W-9), the allocation of proceeds between joint sellers, any principal residence certification (which makes the form unnecessary), and whether a seller is a foreign person. For Forms 1098: the loan servicing records for the year (interest received, outstanding principal on January 1, points, mortgage insurance premiums, any refund of overpaid interest), each loan's origination date and the property securing it, and each borrower's name, SSN and address (from Form W-9S or W-9). For Forms 1099-C and 1099-A: the loan file for each debt (the settlement or write-off with its date and the reason, the principal and interest canceled, whether the borrower was personally liable, a foreclosure's or abandonment's date, the property and its value or bid price) and each debtor's name, TIN and address. For Forms 3921 and 3922: the cap table or equity administration report (Carta, Pulley, the transfer agent's) of the year's ISO exercises or ESPP purchases, with each grant date, exercise (purchase) date, price per share, fair market value per share on the exercise date (and on the grant date for an ESPP), shares and, for an ESPP, the date the shares first went to the employee's brokerage account; and each employee's name, SSN and address from payroll. For a Form 7004: the business's EIN letter or last return (exact name, EIN, address, which return it files, its tax year), the estimated tax payments made for the year, and the user's or their preparer's estimate of the year's total tax. For a Form 990-N: the organization's IRS determination letter or EIN letter (its legal name, EIN, name control and tax year exactly as IRS has them), its mailing address and website, and its principal officer's name and address. For a Form 990-EZ: the same letters, the year's financial statements or books (revenue by source, expenses by kind, the balance sheet at the beginning and end of the year), the list of officers, directors and key employees with their hours and compensation, the program services and what they cost, the governing documents and any changes to them, and the donor records for Schedule B (contributions of $5,000 or more from one contributor). For a Form 8868: the organization's determination letter or EIN letter, which return it extends and the tax year, who keeps its books and where, and for Forms 990-PF, 990-T, 4720 and 6069 the estimate of the year's tax and the estimated tax payments made. For a Form 4868: the taxpayer's name exactly as on their Social Security card, SSN and address (their last Form 1040 shows them), and an estimate of the year's tax and payments (withholding from W-2s and 1099s, estimated tax payments). For a California DE 9: the quarter's payroll register with each employee's subject wages, PIT wages and California PIT withheld, the wages paid each one earlier in the year, the count of employees in the pay period including the 12th of each month, the quarter's DE 88 deposits, the employer payroll tax account number, and the UI and ETT rates on EDD's notice of contribution rates (DE 2088) for the year. For a DE 34: each new or rehired employee's name and SSN as on their W-4, home address and first day of work. For a DE 542: each individual contractor's name, SSN and address (from their W-9), and the contract's start date, amount and end date (or that it is ongoing); a contractor that is a corporation or LLC is not reported. For a DE 88: the payroll register for the payroll date (SDI and California PIT withheld) or the quarter's UI and ETT, the employer's deposit schedule, and the bank account EDD debits (a voided check or bank letter). Read the form with `describe_form` before filling anything: each field's description says what goes in it.
2. Upload every document you read and keep the `key` each one answers. Call `get_return_upload_url`. Send the file the first way you can. With a shell that reaches collietax.com, PUT its bytes to `upload_url`. When this app attaches files to tool calls, save each with `save_return_document`. Otherwise, or when this app's network settings block the PUT, give the user `upload_page_url` (never `upload_url`): a CollieTax page where they pick the file and their browser sends it. Continue once they say it is done: `list_return_uploads` with the `upload_link_id` that came with the URLs gives each document's key. When a blocked PUT sent them there, also tell them once that allowing collietax.com in this app's network settings lets you send files yourself. A PUT carries the file's Content-Type and its name percent-encoded (encodeURIComponent) in an `X-Filename` header, and no other credentials (with curl: `curl -T <file> -H "Content-Type: ..." -H "X-Filename: ..." "<upload_url>"`). The URLs are valid for an hour: after that, get new ones. The same file gives the same key. A return is not prepared without its source documents: if a file cannot be uploaded, tell the user so.
3. Ask the user, in one message, only what the records do not show:
   - for Forms W-2c: the W-2 as filed and what was wrong; for each change the amount as filed and the right amount; why any amount goes down (Form W-3c asks); whether a Form 941-X already adjusted the social security or Medicare tax, and when;
   - for Forms W-2: the contact SSA should reach about the report (name, phone, email); for an employee with tips, the occupation the tips came from (box 14b code); for overtime, only the premium part (the "half" of time-and-a-half) goes in box 12 TT;
   - for Forms 1094-C / 1095-C: whether the plan is insured or self-insured, whether the employer is in an Aggregated ALE Group (and the other members), the affordability safe harbor used (W-2, federal poverty line or rate of pay), and whether it uses the Qualifying Offer or 98% Offer Method; never guess a line 14 or line 16 code, ask;
   - for Forms 1099: a recipient with no W-9 on file (ask the user to get one before January 31; without a TIN the payments are subject to 24% backup withholding); for a contractor paid tips or overtime, the cash tips and the tipped occupation code, and only the overtime premium;
   - for a business's own Form 941, 941-X or 940: who signs Form 8453-EMP for the business (an owner or officer): their name, a daytime phone, and their title as one of IRS's codes for the kind of business (the signer field lists them; a corporation has no CEO code, so ask which code fits, such as CORPORATE OFFICER). Do not ask how they sign: a business filing its own return always signs Form 8453-EMP. A tax professional's client is asked as above;
   - for a Form 941: whether they deposit monthly or semiweekly (their payroll provider knows);
   - if they owe: whether to pay by bank withdrawal with the return (routing and account number, date), or another way;
   - for a Form 941 or 940: if they overpaid, apply it to the next return or get a refund;
   - for a Form 941-X: when they found the errors, whether they want the correction applied to the current quarter or a refund claimed, and which Part 2 statements they can certify;
   - for a Form 940: every state they paid state unemployment tax to, whether any wages were excluded from it or it was paid late, and, for a credit reduction state, the FUTA wages paid there;
   - for a Form 7004: if the tax year is shorter than 12 months, why (first return, last return, change of tax year, joining a consolidated return, or another reason in their words); the estimate of the year's total tax (0 is right for most partnerships and S corporations) and, if a balance is due, whether to pay it now by bank withdrawal; a payment needs the signing officer's name, title, phone and a 5-digit PIN they choose. Tell them the extension gives more time to file, not to pay: tax not paid by the original due date accrues interest and penalties;
   - for a Form 4868: the estimate of the year's total tax and payments, how much they will pay now and how (bank withdrawal with the extension needs a 5-digit PIN they choose; or IRS Direct Pay, EFTPS or a card on their own), whether they will be out of the country on April 15, and whether a declared disaster affected them. Whether they will file a joint return: then the spouse goes on the same Form 4868, and with a payment both sign. To sign, each chooses a 5-digit PIN; for the Self-Select PIN method each also gives their date of birth and last year's AGI exactly as on last year's original return (a wrong AGI gets the extension rejected), otherwise the e-file provider's PIN method needs none of that. The same caution: interest and a late-payment penalty run on tax unpaid by April 15.
   - for a 94x Online Signature PIN Registration: only for a business that files its own Forms 941 and 940, never for a tax professional's client. The authorized signer must be the business's owner or a principal (an officer, a partner, the sole proprietor), signing for this business only: their name, title and SSN, and one or two email addresses; and a contact person (name, title, daytime phone; the signer can be the contact). Tell the user: register at least 45 days before the due date of the first return to be signed with the PIN, and sign with Form 8453-EMP until it is active; IRS mails the PIN with Letter 3083, and the signer activates it by signing the letter's Statement of Receipt and returning it (the letter's Document Upload Tool link is fastest); a business that has a PIN, or a registration in process, cannot register again; a business signing with the PIN may file at most 5 returns a year this way.
   - for a Form 990-N: confirm the organization's gross receipts are normally $50,000 or less (a larger one files Form 990-EZ or 990) and that it is not a private foundation; whether it has terminated (its final e-Postcard); any other names it uses. Tell them it is due by the 15th day of the 5th month after the tax year ends, that it carries no amounts and is not signed, and that an organization that files nothing for 3 years in a row loses its exemption;
   - for a Form 990-EZ: confirm the organization's gross receipts are under $200,000 and its total assets under $500,000 at the end of the year (a larger one files Form 990); the questions of Part V and VI its records do not answer (each element's description says the line); which officer signs Form 8453-TE (their name, title and phone); before filling a schedule, call `describe_form` with that `schedule` and read its elements. Amounts are whole dollars; leave the totals out, the tool figures them. Tell them it is due by the 15th day of the 5th month after the tax year ends, and that the officer signs Form 8453-TE on the review page;
   - for a Form 8868: which return it extends (one Form 8868 per return), whether the tax year is short and why; for a return with tax (990-PF, 990-T, 4720, 6069), the estimate of the tax and payments, and whether to pay the balance now by bank withdrawal: then the officer who signs Form 8453-TE (name, title). Tell them it must be filed by the return's original due date, and that it extends the time to file, not to pay;
   - for a California DE 9: any employee with an approved SDI exclusion (sole shareholder, third-party sick pay, religious), whose wage plan code is R; whether the account is reimbursable for UI (a nonprofit or public employer) or PIT-only; whether the employer went out of business in the quarter, and the date of the final wages;
   - for a California DE 88: the account to debit and the day (a banking day after today, within 90 days; or none, and EDD debits when it processes it), and read both back to them before preparing: EDD takes the money from that account;
   Assume they are an ordinary employer (not a PEO or a section 3504 agent, no research credit) unless they say otherwise.

## Preparing

4. Enter amounts in the unit each field states: integer cents on Forms 941, 941-X, 940, W-2, W-2c, 1099 and 1095-C line 15 and on the DE 9 and DE 88; whole dollars on Forms 7004 and 4868 (but a payment's amountCents is cents). Copy what the records say; never compute a line the tool computes, and never guess a number: ask. One call is one company's returns of one `form`: Forms 941 (one return per quarter) and 941-X are two forms, so are W-2 and W-2c, and each kind of Form 1099 is its own, so a payer with two kinds makes two calls; another company is another call. A form filed per person (W-2, W-2c, 1094-C, 1099) is one return with every person in it. Put each return's facts in its `input`, list its uploaded keys in `source_files`, and give it a `ref` (the quarter, say) to match its result.
5. A call saves all its returns or none. When `saved` is false nothing was saved: for each return whose `outcome` is `needs_input`, explain each of its `errors` in plain words, ask the user, and call again with every return of the call, the `ok` ones too. Show `warnings` too (1099: a recipient paid under the box's threshold, $2,000 for most, needs no form, one without a TIN faces backup withholding, a form past its due date; W-2: amounts that do not match the tax rates), which the user should confirm against the payroll records; they do not block the form.
6. When `saved` is true, tell the user the key lines (for a 941: total taxes after adjustments, deposits, balance due or overpayment; for a 941-X: the total, line 27 (line 19a on the April 2027 revision, which corrects 2027 quarters); for a 940: the FUTA tax after adjustments, deposits, balance due or overpayment; for a 7004: the return extended, the tax year, and lines 6 to 8; for a 4868: the name and SSN as printed, and lines 4 to 7; for a 990-N: the organization's name, EIN and tax year as printed, which must match IRS's records; for a 990-EZ: lines 9 (total revenue), 17 (total expenses), 18 and 21 (net assets at the end of the year); for an 8868: the return extended, the tax year, and lines 3a to 3c; for W-2s: the Form W-3 totals in boxes 1 to 8, and that boxes 2, 3, 5 and 7 agree with the year's four Forms 941; for Forms 1095-C: each employee's line 14 and 16 codes month by month; for Forms 1099: each recipient's name, TIN and amounts, and the totals; tell them the review page also gives each recipient's copy (Copy B), to send them by January 31; for a DE 9: total subject wages, the UI, ETT and SDI amounts, PIT withheld, deposits and balance due, and that the DE 9C's wage items match the payroll register; for a DE 34 or DE 542: each person's name, SSN and dates, and any `warnings` about reports already past the 20 days; for a DE 88: the two amounts, the total, the account's last four digits and the debit date) and ask them to check them against their records. Give the user the batch's `review_url`: they read the printed forms there after signing in, sign Form 8453-EMP for a business's own Form 941, 941-X or 940 (or Form 8453-TE, the officer, for an organization's own Form 990-EZ, or its Form 8868 with a payment), and confirm the batch.
7. A saved batch never changes. To change anything: read it with `get_return_batch` and `include_input` (it gives back the call that made it), ask the user before withdrawing a batch they signed or confirmed, withdraw it with `withdraw_batch`, and make the call again with the change, which gives a new review link to pass on. A user holds one live original per company, form, period and person (a quarter for Forms 941 and DE 9; a day for a DE 34 or DE 542, so put everyone of one day's report in one return; the payroll date and tax type for a DE 88; a year for the others, and for a Form 7004 also the return it extends). Corrections (Forms 941-X, W-2c) can follow one another. Once the agency has answered (`get_return_batch` shows a rejected batch or return with its `fix_by`, or accepted ones), a change is a new call with `follows` set to that batch: a batch rejected whole is replaced whole, rejected returns are sent again as the same form, and accepted ones are corrected with Form 941-X, Form W-2c or the same Form 1099, the call holding only the quarters or people it changes. A follow-up the agency rejected in turn is followed in its own turn, with `follows` naming its batch (`FOLLOW_UP_EXISTS` names it). Forms 940, 1094-C, 1095-C and California's are not corrected here yet.
8. A return whose `outcome` is `refused` was not prepared as sent: `RETURN_IN_BATCH` means it is already in a batch not sent (withdraw that batch first, if the user wants it changed); `RETURN_FILED` that it was sent or accepted (its message says what a change is instead); `DUPLICATE_IN_CALL` that the call listed it twice; `NOT_IN_FOLLOWED`, `NOT_CORRECTED_LIKE_THIS` and `FOLLOW_UP_EXISTS` that it is not a follow-up of the batch in `follows` as sent (its message says what to do instead). Tell the user what it says.
9. To find what was prepared before this conversation, use `list_return_batches` (by company with `payer_id`, by form or by status), then `get_return_batch`.
